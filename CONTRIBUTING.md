# Contributing to Guardian Escolar

Thank you for your interest in contributing to Guardian Escolar.

Guardian Escolar is a proprietary software project developed and maintained by the `school-guardian-project` organization. We welcome contributions from developers and collaborators who wish to help improve the platform, its services, documentation, and technical infrastructure.

Our contribution model encourages collaboration while preserving the project's intellectual property, architectural integrity, security, and long-term sustainability.

By participating in the project, contributors agree to follow the guidelines described in this document and the applicable organizational policies.

## 1. Contribution Principles

All contributions must follow these principles:

- **Controlled collaboration:** Contributions are accepted from authorized collaborators through the established review process.
- **Code integrity:** Changes must respect the existing architecture, design decisions, and development standards.
- **Security first:** Every contribution must consider security, data protection, and the reliability of the platform.
- **Traceability:** Contributions must maintain a verifiable record of their authorship and integration history.
- **Recognition:** Authors of accepted contributions will receive appropriate recognition.
- **Intellectual property protection:** Contributions are subject to the project's applicable intellectual property agreements and policies.

## 2. Access and Authorization

Guardian Escolar does not operate under an unrestricted public contribution model.

Access to source code repositories is granted exclusively to authorized collaborators.

Before receiving access, contributors may be required to:

- Obtain authorization from the project maintainers.
- Accept the applicable Contributor Agreement and intellectual property conditions.
- Review the development, security, and repository policies.
- Receive access permissions appropriate to their assigned responsibilities.

Access to one repository does not automatically grant access to other repositories within the organization.

## 3. Contribution Workflow

All code contributions must follow the established workflow:

1. **Proposal:** Identify the problem, improvement, or functionality to be addressed.
2. **Evaluation:** The maintainers assess the proposal's relevance, technical feasibility, and potential impact.
3. **Authorization:** The contributor receives authorization and the necessary repository access.
4. **Development:** Changes are implemented in an independent working branch.
5. **Pull Request:** The contributor submits the changes for review.
6. **Review:** Authorized reviewers evaluate code quality, architecture, testing, security, and compatibility.
7. **Approval:** An authorized maintainer approves the proposed changes.
8. **Integration:** Approved changes are merged into the appropriate target branch.

Submitting a contribution does not guarantee its acceptance or integration.

## 4. Branching and Repository Rules

Contributors must follow the branching strategy established by each repository.

The following rules apply:

- Direct commits to protected branches are prohibited.
- Changes must be developed in separate working branches.
- Pull Requests are mandatory for integrating changes.
- Contributors must not bypass required reviews or automated checks.
- Branches must be kept reasonably up to date with their target branches.
- Repository-specific rules take precedence when they impose additional requirements.

Example branch naming conventions:

- `feature/description`
- `fix/description`
- `security/description`
- `docs/description`
- `refactor/description`

### 4.1 Forking and Redistribution

Guardian Escolar does not authorize independent forks, redistribution, or the creation of independently maintained copies of its proprietary source code.

Contributions must be developed through authorized access to the organization's repositories and submitted using the established Pull Request workflow.

No contributor may independently publish, distribute, sublicense, or commercially exploit Guardian Escolar or derivative versions without express authorization from the rights holder.

## 5. Code Quality and Technical Standards

All contributions must:

- Follow the applicable language and framework conventions.
- Respect the project's architectural boundaries and established design decisions.
- Maintain consistency with existing coding standards.
- Include appropriate tests when applicable.
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies and duplicated functionality.
- Include relevant documentation updates.
- Avoid introducing hardcoded credentials, secrets, or sensitive information.
- Avoid introducing known vulnerabilities or insecure implementation patterns.

Contributors must consult `DEVELOPMENT-STANDARDS.md` and `ARCHITECTURE-POLICY.md` where applicable.

## 6. Pull Request Requirements

Every Pull Request must provide sufficient information for technical evaluation.

It should include:

- A clear and descriptive title.
- The problem being addressed.
- A summary of the implemented changes.
- The affected services, modules, or components.
- Relevant tests and validation results.
- Potential architectural or security implications.
- Related issues or technical proposals, where applicable.

Pull Requests may be returned for modifications, placed on hold, or rejected when they do not satisfy the project's requirements.

## 7. Review and Approval

All contributions are subject to review by authorized maintainers.

Depending on the nature of the change, the review may involve:

- Technical and architectural review.
- Code quality assessment.
- Automated testing.
- Security and dependency analysis.
- Compatibility and integration verification.
- Documentation review.

Only authorized maintainers may approve and integrate changes into protected branches.

The original contributor's authorship must be preserved in the contribution history whenever technically and legally applicable.

## 8. Recognition of Contributors

Guardian Escolar values the work and participation of its contributors.

Authors of accepted contributions may be recognized through:

- Git commit authorship.
- Pull Request attribution.
- The organization's `CONTRIBUTORS.md` file, where maintained.
- Release notes and changelogs, when relevant.

Recognition is intended to acknowledge the actual work performed and does not, by itself, grant ownership of the project, commercial rights, or participation in its revenues.

## 9. Voluntary Contributions and Compensation

Unless a separate written agreement establishes otherwise, participation in the project's external contribution program is voluntary and does not provide automatic entitlement to:

- Salaries or wages.
- Professional fees or honoraria.
- Royalties or licensing revenue.
- Commercial profits or revenue sharing.
- Equity or ownership interests in the project.

This provision does not override applicable employment, contractual, or other legal obligations arising from the actual circumstances of a contributor's relationship with the project.

## 10. Intellectual Property and Contributor Agreements

All contributions must comply with the intellectual property requirements established by Guardian Escolar.

Before a contribution is accepted, contributors must satisfy the applicable Contributor Agreement, CLA, or other written authorization required by the project.

Submitting code does not automatically transfer ownership of intellectual property.

The applicable written agreement determines the rights granted or transferred, the permitted exploitation modalities, and the relevant territorial and temporal scope.

Authors' moral rights must be respected in accordance with applicable law.

## 11. Security and Confidentiality

Contributors must protect all non-public information accessed during their participation.

The following activities are prohibited without explicit authorization:

- Disclosing confidential source code or internal documentation.
- Publishing credentials, tokens, keys, or configuration secrets.
- Sharing private infrastructure information.
- Disclosing vulnerabilities through unauthorized public channels.
- Accessing systems, data, or repositories beyond granted permissions.
- Using project information for unauthorized purposes.

Security vulnerabilities must be reported according to the procedures established in `SECURITY.md`.

## 12. Rejection and Revocation of Access

Guardian Escolar reserves the right to reject contributions that do not meet its technical, security, legal, or organizational requirements.

Repository access may also be suspended or revoked when necessary to protect the project's infrastructure, intellectual property, or operational integrity, subject to applicable agreements and law.

## 13. Scope and Interpretation

This document establishes the general contribution workflow for the `school-guardian-project` organization.

Individual repositories may define additional technical requirements, provided they do not conflict with the organization's governing policies.

In case of conflict, applicable written agreements and higher-level organizational policies shall govern, subject to applicable law.
